# Research: "SOCK compliance" (SOC 2) for DocForge

Asked for on 2026-10-03 (a friend suggested it). Researched by an agent from web sources; the
figures are from vendor and consultancy pages, not official price lists.

## What it is

"SOCK" is SOC 2.

- **Not a certificate and not a law.** It is an attestation report: a licensed US CPA firm gives its opinion of a company's security controls against the AICPA's Trust Services Criteria.
- **The five criteria:** Security is mandatory. Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality and Privacy are optional.
- **Type I and Type II:**
  - **Type I:** the controls are designed well at one point in time.
  - **Type II:** they also worked over an observation window, usually 3–12 months. Enterprise buyers usually want Type II.
- **SOC 1:** controls over a customer's financial reporting. It is relevant later if customers' auditors rely on DocForge's payment-approval drafts.
- **SOC 3:** a public summary of a SOC 2.

**Wording:**
- **Before a report exists:** "our controls are designed around the SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria; we have not yet had a SOC 2 examination."
- **After a report exists:** "we have a SOC 2 Type II report covering [period], available under NDA."
- **Never:** "SOC 2 certified".

## Cost and time for a solo, pre-revenue company (2025–2026, indicative)

| Item | Cost | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Groundwork done yourself (policies, MFA, backups, vendor list) | about $0, plus your time | 2–6 weeks |
| Automation platform (Sprinto, Secureframe, Drata, Vanta) | about $6k–14k a year | weeks to set up |
| Type I audit, boutique CPA firm | about $5k–15k | weeks |
| Type II audit, boutique CPA firm | about $7k–20k (typical $15k–50k) | 3–12-month window plus a 4–8-week report |
| Penetration test | a few thousand dollars | 1–3 weeks |
| **First-year SOC 2 Type II, total** | **about $15k–40k** | **6–12 months** |
| ISO 27001 in India (one consultancy's figures) | ₹3–5L in year one; the certificate lasts 3 years | 12–16 weeks |

**Caution:** a 2026 whistleblower accused one fast, cheap SOC 2 platform of templated reports and questionable auditors. These are allegations, not established facts. Check that an auditor is a licensed, peer-reviewed CPA firm.

## Does DocForge need it now?

No.

- **No customers yet.** A report needs a running production system to observe, and the cost is high.
- **India first.** Indian procurement more often names ISO 27001. SOC 2 is mostly asked for by US buyers. Nothing was found on what Indian pharma distributors require of software suppliers.
- **Pharma buyers.** They also qualify suppliers under GxP. SOC 2 does not replace the validation-support pack (`docs/validation/`), which stays the lead document for them.

**Already in place, mapped to the criteria:**
- **Logical access:** row-level security, hashed credentials, lockout, least-privilege IAM, secrets in SSM.
- **Encryption.**
- **Monitoring:** the hash-chained audit log, alarms and the ops check.
- **Change management:** CI, the eval gate, Terraform, images pinned by digest.
- **Processing integrity:** citations, rules and review. This is unusually strong.

**Main gaps:**
- written policies;
- backups and disaster recovery (the demo has none);
- an incident response plan;
- a vendor and subprocessor register;
- evidence of access reviews and MFA everywhere;
- segregation of duties (one person; compensating controls instead);
- HR controls;
- an organisational risk register and a penetration test;
- customer terms, a data processing agreement and a privacy notice.

**One gap that matters before any real customer data, whatever the certification:** use Gemini through Vertex AI, or at least the paid API, for real data. The free AI Studio tier is outside Google's SOC 2 and ISO 27001 scope, and may be used for training.

## Recommendation

1. **Now (almost free):**
   - Write about ten short policies.
   - Turn on MFA everywhere and lock the AWS root account.
   - Enable CloudTrail.
   - Make branch protection apply to admins.
   - Keep a vendor register.
   - Write an incident plan and a backup and disaster recovery plan.
   - Publish an honest security page.
   - Answer a standard questionnaire (CAIQ Lite or SIG Lite) once, to reuse.
   - Start keeping evidence (dated access reviews).
   - Move to Vertex AI before real data.
2. **At first customers:**
   - Answer questionnaires, and offer the validation pack and a supplier-audit call.
   - If an Indian enterprise makes certification a condition, get ISO 27001 first.
   - If a US client requires SOC 2: an entry-tier platform plus a peer-reviewed CPA firm. Type I to unblock the deal, then Type II.
3. **At scale:**
   - A yearly SOC 2 Type II, a public SOC 3, and ISO 27001 as well.
   - SOC 1 if customers' financial auditors rely on DocForge.
   - A second person, for real segregation of duties.
